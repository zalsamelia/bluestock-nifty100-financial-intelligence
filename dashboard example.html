Rate of Sea Level Rise

Satellite measurements and tidal gauge stations consistently show escalating sea level anomalies. For regions with extremely low topography such as Tuvalu and Kiribati, every millimeter of rise significantly amplifies the daily tidal flooding threat.

Sea Surface Temperature & Sea Level Anomaly

Global warming is driving an exponential rise in Pacific Ocean surface temperatures. The direct consequences are thermal expansion of seawater and accelerated melting of polar glaciers, causing sea levels to rise to historically unprecedented levels.

03 Extreme Rainfall Is Becoming More Frequent
03

Instability of Rainfall Cycles

Warmer ocean temperatures accelerate evaporation across the Pacific atmosphere. The result is an increasingly volatile hydrological cycle: daily rainfall exceeding normal thresholds triggers flash flooding, while dry periods grow progressively longer and more severe.

04 Islands Are Shrinking
04

Coastal Erosion & Shoreline Change

The combination of elevated sea levels and persistent wave action is intensifying coastal erosion. Aerial and satellite imagery reveals that once-extensive sandy beach areas have been submerged, forcing a measurable contraction of island landmass over recent decades.

05 Climate Risk Index & Social Impact
05

Vulnerability & Social Consequences

The impacts of climate change are not distributed equally. The Climate Risk Index integrates sea level rise rates, coastline erosion levels, coastal population density, and life-sustaining infrastructure to produce a composite vulnerability score.

About the Risk Index: Risk scores are calculated from a composite of: (1) the rate of sea level rise, (2) percentage of coastline lost, (3) coastal population density, and (4) frequency of annual extreme rainfall events.
